How to Get Traffic — The Boring Way

The offers on the reviews page are useless without traffic. This guide covers free, honest methods to get eyes on your affiliate links. None of them are fast. None of them are exciting. All of them work if you stick with them for 3–6 months.

Reality check: If you need money this week, affiliate marketing won't help. The methods below take months to compound. If you need cash now, get a job or sell something. If you want to build a slow, self-sustaining income stream, keep reading.

The problem

You can have the best affiliate offer in the world. If nobody sees your link, you earn nothing. Traffic is the real bottleneck — not the product, not the commission rate, not the sales page. Getting traffic is the hard part, and it's where 90% of people give up.

Method 1: SEO blog content (the main path)

Write honest answers to questions people are already searching for. This site is an example — "earn extra online", "best sleep aid review", "does [product] actually work". Google sends free traffic to pages that answer questions better than the competition.

How to do it

  • Pick one niche — like sleep, fitness, or money-making — and stick to it. Don't jump around.
  • Find questions people search for. Use Google's autocomplete (type "how to sleep" and see what appears), or free tools like AnswerThePublic.
  • Write 800–1500 words per article answering the question honestly. No fluff, no padding.
  • Include your affiliate link naturally — when you mention the product, link to it. Don't shove it in.
  • Publish 2–3 articles per week for 3–6 months. Consistency matters more than quality of any single post.

This is exactly what this site does. The review pages answer "does this product work?" and the guide pages answer "how do I get traffic to it?" Both rank for search terms and both contain affiliate links.

Method 2: YouTube (the trust accelerator)

A video review ranks faster than a blog post because YouTube has less competition in most niches. You don't need a camera — a screen recording of the product's sales page with your voice over it works fine.

How to do it

  • Record a 5–10 minute honest review of the product. Show the sales page, walk through the claims, say what's real and what's hype.
  • Put your affiliate link in the description (first line, above the fold).
  • Title it for search: "YU SLEEP Review — Does It Actually Work?" not "My Honest Thoughts on a Sleep Product".
  • Publish 1 video per week. Consistency beats quality in the first 6 months.

Method 3: Pinterest (the slow-burn visual play)

Pinterest is a search engine, not a social network. Pins live for months and keep getting clicks long after you post them. For sleep, fitness, and health niches, Pinterest sends steady traffic.

How to do it

  • Create simple pins in Canva (free). One image, one headline, one short description.
  • Link each pin to your review page or a specific blog post.
  • Pin 3–5 pins per day. Schedule them with Pinterest's free scheduler.
  • Use keywords in pin titles and descriptions — Pinterest search is keyword-driven.

Method 4: Forums and community participation

Reddit, Facebook groups, niche forums. Don't spam your link — answer questions honestly, build a reputation, and link to your review when it's genuinely relevant. This is slow but it brings high-quality traffic (people who already trust you).

How to do it

  • Find 3–5 active communities in your niche. r/Sleep, r/fitness, Facebook sleep groups.
  • Spend 20 minutes a day answering questions without linking to anything.
  • After 2–3 weeks, start linking to your reviews when someone asks about a specific product.
  • Never link in every post. You'll get banned. Link 1 in 5 posts, maximum.

What NOT to do

  • Don't pay for ads until you've proven the offer converts with free traffic. You'll burn money.
  • Don't spam your link in comments, forums, or DMs. It gets you banned and destroys trust.
  • Don't chase shiny objects. Pick one niche, one traffic method, and stick with it for 6 months before adding anything new.
  • Don't expect fast results. Month 1: nothing. Month 2: a few clicks. Month 3–6: it starts compounding if you stayed consistent.

The realistic timeline

Timeframe What to expect
Month 1 Site built, 8–12 articles published, 4 YouTube videos. Traffic: near zero. Don't panic.
Month 2–3 Google starts indexing. A few clicks per day. Maybe one or two affiliate clicks. Still no sales — normal.
Month 4–6 Search rankings improve. 20–50 visitors/day if you stayed consistent. First sales start appearing.
Month 6–12 If you published 2–3 articles/week consistently, you could see $5–$20/day. This is the compounding phase.
These numbers are estimates, not promises. I have not hit these numbers yet on this site. They're based on what's publicly known about affiliate marketing with consistent content output. Your results depend on your niche, your content quality, and whether you actually stick with it.

Next step

Pick one traffic method. Just one. Write or record content for it consistently for 3 months. Come back to the reviews page when you have traffic — that's where the money is.
